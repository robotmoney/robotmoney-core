# Quantity pricing shields holders where NAV fails

The one-vault fund on Base should price deposits by quantity, not by NAV: USDC buys a pro-rata slice of every holding inside the vault, shares mint by the smallest quantity ratio of what actually arrived, and no price enters the share math. The evidence is lopsided. Across 38 share-pricing incidents from 2020 to 2026, **23 hit designs that read a price or computed NAV in the flow (about $418M gross), 6 hit internal exchange-rate designs (about $238M), and zero losses were found on a strictly in-kind pro-rata mint or redeem path**. The NAV alternative cannot be hardened for this fund today, because the hardening recipe (TWAP plus independent cross-check plus delay plus rate limit) needs an independent price, and deSPXA has no Chainlink feed on Base, a few million dollars of DEX depth, and a once-a-day issuer NAV. Traditional finance already ran this experiment: mutual funds that dealt at a stale NAV lost 56 to 114 basis points a year to arbitrageurs, and ETFs removed the problem by dealing in kind and making the flow pay its own trading cost. NAV-priced vaults do hold the TVL in 2026 (Veda, Maple, Lagoon, Upshift), but they hold it by trusting an off-chain accountant, which this fund does not need for flows. Quantity pricing does not remove price risk; it relocates it to the depositor's own swap (bounded by a minimum-shares floor) and to the governed rebalance, which is where the oracle-grade hardening belongs. The hybrid worth adding is narrow: an optional batched deposit queue that executes when deSPXA liquidity exists, plus oracle checks that can only revert a trade and never set a share count. The design has no direct precedent, since every quantity-based protocol found puts the single-asset entry in a periphery zap, so the in-vault purchase path carries the audit burden.

## Two classes, three NAV families, and a handful of hybrids

The dividing line is whether a number that someone can move or let go stale is converted into a share count. Quantity-based designs compute only `amount_i = balance_i × shares / totalSupply` for every constituent. NAV-based designs divide a valuation by supply, and they split into three families by where the valuation comes from: an off-chain accountant pushing a rate, on-chain oracles read at call time, or internal accounting of a single asset.

**Class (a): quantity-based, in-kind, no oracle in the mint or redeem path**

| Protocol | Mint | Redeem | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uniswap v2 | `min(amount0·supply/reserve0, amount1·supply/reserve1)` | pro-rata | Credits the balance delta that arrived; excess of one token is a gift to holders; locks `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY` on first mint ([source](https://github.com/Uniswap/v2-core/blob/master/contracts/UniswapV2Pair.sol)) |
| Balancer v3 (proportional add/remove only) | exact BPT out, proportional in | proportional, "zero price impact" | Unbalanced and single-token kinds are invariant-priced, not quantity-priced ([docs](https://docs.balancer.fi/concepts/vault/add-remove-liquidity-types.html)) |
| Curve (`remove_liquidity`, balanced adds) | balanced add pays no imbalance fee (one pool audit) | every coin pro-rata, no fee | ([docs](https://curve.readthedocs.io/exchange-pools.html), [audit](https://reports.chainsecurity.com/Curve/ChainSecurity_Curve_CurveETHSETH_Audit.pdf)) |
| Set Protocol v2 / Index Coop (Basic and Debt issuance modules) | exact shares out, `preciseMulCeil` units in | `preciseMul` (floor) | Reads stored position units; rejects external positions ([source](https://github.com/SetProtocol/set-protocol-v2/blob/master/contracts/protocol/modules/v1/BasicIssuanceModule.sol)) |

| Alongside AMKT | custodian-verified in-kind | in-kind | Per 2023 whitepaper only ([whitepaper](https://data.kando.tech/sites/default/files/2023-09/Alongside%20Whitepaper.pdf)) |

**Class (b): NAV-based**

| Family | Protocols | How the price is set |
|---|---|---|
| Pushed rate from an off-chain accountant | Veda BoringVault (and ether.fi Liquid on it), Lagoon, Mellow Core / Lido Earn, Upshift (and Kelp Gain on it), Ondo OUSG, Centrifuge, Ribbon | Veda's accountant pauses on a too-early or out-of-bounds update ([source](https://github.com/Se7en-Seas/boring-vault/blob/main/src/base/Roles/AccountantWithRateProviders.sol)); Lagoon needs a valuation provider to propose and a curator to settle ([docs](https://docs.lagoon.finance/vault/vault-valuation.md)); Mellow reverts far-off reports and flags moderate ones for human acceptance ([docs](https://docs.lido.fi/earn/architecture/oracle)); Centrifuge managers post a price per epoch ([guide](https://developer.centrifuge.io/developer/protocol/guides/manage-a-pool/)) |
| On-chain oracle at call time | Enzyme (deposits), dHEDGE / Toros, Sommelier, Makina, GMX GLP and GM, Jupiter JLP | Enzyme values every position through a shared `ValueInterpreter` on Chainlink and Redstone feeds ([specs](https://specs.enzyme.finance/topics/position-pricing)); dHEDGE prices at deposit time with a 24-hour lockup ([docs](https://docs.dhedge.org/dhedge-protocol/depositing-into-a-vault)); JLP divides oracle-valued pool assets by supply ([docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/earn/jlp/earn)) |
| Internal accounting, single asset | Yearn v3, MetaMorpho, Maple / Syrup | Yearn tracks idle and debt internally "to avoid price_per_share manipulation" and unlocks profit linearly ([docs](https://docs.yearn.fi/developers/smart-contracts/V3/VaultV3)); Maple exits at `(totalAssets − unrealizedLosses) / totalSupply` at processing time ([docs](https://docs.maple.finance/technical-resources/withdrawal-managers/withdrawal-manager-queue)) |

Three protocols straddle the line, and each is instructive. **Enzyme mints at oracle NAV but redeems in kind by pure balance ratio**, wrapping the NAV call in try/catch so that a failing price cannot block exit ([source](https://github.com/enzymefinance/protocol/blob/v4/contracts/release/core/fund/comptroller/ComptrollerLib.sol)). Reserve RTokens price by quantity but gate on oracle-judged basket health. Indexed Finance held its basket in kind but allowed single-asset AMM joins and sized a re-index from its own spot state, and that side door is what was drained. One structural fact matters for the recommendation: the notes found **no live protocol that takes a single stablecoin, buys the pro-rata slice inside the core contract and mints by quantity ratio**. Index Coop's FlashMint and Reserve's Zapper both sit outside the core as periphery contracts ([FlashMintDex](https://github.com/IndexCoop/index-coop-smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/exchangeIssuance/FlashMintDex.sol), [Reserve docs](https://reserve-docs.pages.dev/reserve-index/mint-redeem)). The search was not exhaustive, so this is absence of evidence.

## Zero losses on in-kind paths against $418M on price-reading paths

Hardness is where the classes separate most cleanly. The incident review counted 23 exploits of NAV or oracle-priced flows for about $418M, 6 exploits of internal exchange-rate designs for about $238M ($197M of it Euler), 4 AMM invariant-math failures for $328M to $361M, and none on a strict in-kind mint or redeem. Every loss above $1M ran through a line of code that turned a manipulable number (pool spot, LP virtual price, `totalAssets/totalSupply`, computed AUM, an invariant) into shares or debt. The zero for quantity designs is partly a base-rate effect, since far less value sits in strict in-kind funds than in ERC-4626 vaults and lending markets. It is evidence of structural safety on that path, not proof.

The NAV failures are not confined to naive designs. Harvest lost $33.8M in 2020 by valuing Curve holdings at spot with same-transaction deposit and withdrawal, moving share price only about 1% per cycle and simply repeating ([post-mortem](https://medium.com/harvest-finance/harvest-flashloan-economic-attack-post-mortem-3cf900d65217)). Six years later the same shape recurred twice. **Makina lost about $4.13M on 2026-01-20 when an accounting refresh read a flash-loan-skewed Curve value and share price jumped from about 1.01 to 1.33 in one transaction** ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/blog/makina-incident-analysis), [Verichains](https://blog.verichains.io/p/analyzing-the-4m-makina-finance-exploit)). **Summer.fi lost $6.04M on 2026-07-06 when a donation of vault tokens still carried at a stale pre-collapse mark lifted share price 9.5%** and the attacker redeemed against liquid strategies ([Verichains](https://blog.verichains.io/p/lazy-summer-hack-analysis-stale-asset)). GMX v1 lost $40M to $42M in 2025 with honest external prices, because reentrancy corrupted internal state that the AUM formula consumed ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/gmx-incident-cross-contract-reentrancy-bypasses-a-four-year-old-guard)). A NAV formula therefore has two attack surfaces, the prices it reads and its own state, and oracle hardening covers only the first.

Hardening raises the attacker's cost without closing the door when the reference market is thin. Inverse Finance had a TWAP feed and lost $15.6M after 500 ETH moved a thin Sushi pool about 50-fold ([analysis](https://kyrianalex.substack.com/p/the-inverse-finance-hack?s=r)). Rari's pool 23 fell to own-capital manipulation of a thin Uniswap v3 pool held across blocks ([Silo](https://www.silo.finance/post/history-of-exploits-rari-capital)). UwU Lend used a median of 11 sources, 5 of them manipulable ([Neptune Mutual](https://medium.com/neptune-mutual/understanding-the-uwu-lend-exploit-b32ea552f030)). The closest match to the hardening package under consideration is **Venus in March 2026: a dual-feed BoundValidator rejected a manipulated price for about 37 minutes, then accepted it once both feeds converged on the same thin market** ([Venus post-mortem](https://community.venus.io/t/the-market-incident-post-mortem/5712)). Delays and async settlement would have stopped every atomic case in the set. They would not have stopped Mango, Inverse, Rari, Venus or the Stream Finance collapse, where an issuer-reported share price stayed high while backing was impaired and at least $284.9M of debt exposure was traced ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/defi-sleuths-trace-284m-stream-finance-exposure)). Jump Crypto adds that time delays can backfire in volatile markets ([Jump Crypto](https://jumpcrypto.com/writing/so-you-still-want-to-use-a-price-oracle/)).

Quantity designs fail at their edges instead. Indexed Finance lost about $16M in October 2021 through swaps and a re-index step that priced from its own manipulated state, not through a pro-rata join or exit ([post-mortem](https://hackmd.io/@laurenceday/HJVIP-PHY), [BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/the-analysis-of-indexed-finance-security-incident)). Set lost about $49,000 to a compromised manager hot wallet ([Set Protocol](https://medium.com/set-protocol/addressing-the-usdapy-and-btcapy-sets-exploit-3418fa722d99)). Balancer's 2025 loss ($94.8M per Balancer) came from rounding on an exact-out swap path, while proportional joins and exits were untouched ([Balancer post-mortem](https://medium.com/balancer-protocol/nov-3-exploit-post-mortem-51dcbeb6b020)). The attack surface that remains for a strict in-kind vault is short: the rebalance (holders lose), manager keys (holders lose), the zap or swap leg (only the flow participant loses, because the vault receives exact quantities), rounding amplified by tiny repeated calls (Bunni's 44 micro-withdrawals are the precedent in AMM math, with no in-kind vault precedent found) ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/bunni-incident-repeated-small-withdrawals-compound-a-rounding-error-into-an-8.4m-drain)), and third-party lenders that price the share by its exchange rate (the lender loses, as at Cream, Venus wUSDM, Resupply and LlamaLend). Donations, the classic ERC-4626 weapon, become a pro-rata gift to holders. No auditor source recommends in-kind pricing by name; the case for it is an inference from this loss record.

## NAV designs hold the TVL while quantity designs hold the record

Adoption runs the other way, and it would be dishonest to wave that off. On 2026-11-10, pushed-rate and internally accounted vaults dominated: **Maple about $2.23B, Veda about $1.68B (peak $6.1B in September 2025), Concrete about $1.17B, Upshift $481M, Mellow Core $247M, Lagoon $155M** ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/protocols)). Curated vault value was put at $7.18B across 55 curators in August 2026 ([Sentora](https://defillama.com/research/report/sentora-the-vault-economy-scale-and-composition)). Call-time multi-asset NAV funds, the family nearest to this fund's shape, are small or dying: Enzyme $87M, Toros $4M, Sommelier $1M. Quantity-based index products collapsed harder. **Set and Index Coop fell from about $550M to $600M in November 2021 to about $11M** ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/index-coop)), and **Reserve fell from $530M in September 2025 to $45M** ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/reserve-protocol)). Quantity pricing keeps real scale only in AMMs, with Uniswap v2 at $818M and Curve at $1.06B ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/uniswap-v2), [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/curve-dex)).

The reasons the teams gave matter more than the totals. Index Coop's IIP-190 cited 13 straight quarters of negative net growth for DPI, early traction that relied on "extremely heavy liquidity mining incentives", and high maintenance cost; it did not blame the issuance math ([IIP-190](https://gov.indexcoop.com/t/iip-190-strategic-realignment-discontinuing-support-for-legacy-products/4856)). The cost that did bite was rebalancing: a governance post reported 100 to 200 trades and $20,000 to $40,000 of mainnet gas per DPI or MVI rebalance, a figure seen only through a search summary ([forum](https://gov.indexcoop.com/t/product-performance-surfacing-gas-costs/3209)). Both major lineages replaced manager DEX trades with Dutch auctions for that reason ([Index Coop](https://indexcoop.com/blog/introducing-auction-rebalancing), [Reserve docs](https://reserve-209.pages.dev/protocol/index_dtfs/protocol_operations)). The fair reading is that in-kind issuance is not what killed index products, and pushed-rate NAV is not what made Veda large; demand for the strategies did both.

| Criterion | Quantity-based (a) | NAV-based (b) |
|---|---|---|
| Usefulness | Poor native UX: multi-token in and out, so single-asset entry needs a zap; ERC-4626 previews do not apply cleanly; cannot hold non-transferable positions through in-kind transfers (Set and FlashMint reject external positions) | Best UX: one asset in, one transaction, composable as collateral (syrupUSDC on Aave, Veda shares through capped-rate wrappers); async variants break `preview*` ([EIP-7540](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7540)) |
| Correctness | Exact by construction: every share holds the same quantities before and after a flow, with ceil-on-mint and floor-on-redeem rounding; a stale or wrong price cannot be exploited through the mint | Only as correct as the mark: Enzyme states that deviation thresholds under 1% "could result in significant arbitrage opportunities" in a large fund and that a share lock does not stop existing holders redeeming at a stale high ([Enzyme specs](https://specs.enzyme.finance/topics/known-risks-and-mitigations)) |
| Adoption | Large in AMMs, shrinking in funds | Dominant in curated vaults, via a trusted accountant |
| Hardness | No loss found on the in-kind path; losses at rebalance, keys and periphery | 23 incidents and about $418M, including cases with TWAPs, medians and bound validators in place |

The slippage allocation is the property that makes quantity pricing correct rather than merely safe. The zapper pays for every swap and the pool receives exact pro-rata quantities, so a large deposit cannot dilute holders through bad execution. FlashMint bounds only the aggregate input (each component swap carries an unlimited maximum), which means one thin constituent can consume the budget and force a revert in which the user loses gas alone ([FlashMintDex](https://github.com/IndexCoop/index-coop-smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/exchangeIssuance/FlashMintDex.sol)). Reserve reports that zaps leave about 1 to 10 bps of input as dust ([Reserve docs](https://reserve-docs.pages.dev/reserve-index/mint-redeem)). Those are the measured costs of the pattern, and they fall on the person who chose to transact.

## Mutual funds bled 56 to 114 basis points; ETFs fixed it in kind

Traditional finance supplies the measured cost of each choice. A fund that deals at one NAV hands every trader a free option whenever that NAV is stale or predictable. **Zitzewitz measured dilution of long-term holders in international equity funds at 56 bps a year in 1998-99, rising to 114 bps in 2001, with arbitrageurs earning 35% to 70% a year** ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/working-papers/who-cares-about-shareholders-arbitrage-proofing-mutual-funds)). Dilution scaled with staleness, 98 bps a year in the stalest quintile against 18 bps in the freshest ([Qian](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2008-Athens/papers/Qian.pdf)). The same paper found that funds were slow to fix a known problem and that slowness tracked board insiders and expense ratios, an agency failure and not a technical one. A vault that mints at a NAV frozen for one holding over a weekend recreates the international-fund trade exactly, and on-chain it meets no trade-frequency limits and bots that watch index futures against the frozen mark.

The repair kit had three parts, each closing a different leak: forward pricing so that nobody trades on a known price, fair-value adjustment so that nobody trades on a stale one, and swing pricing or levies so that the flow pays its trading cost. Swing pricing is the weakest of the three in practice. The SEC made it optional in 2016 with a 2% cap, proposed a mandate in 2022 and dropped it in 2024 ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-issues-final-rule-investment-company-swing-pricing), [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/industry-news/news/sec-scraps-swing-pricing-plan-for-mutual-funds-256824)). In Luxembourg bond funds during March 2020, swing factors rose by more than 100 bps yet had no significant effect on outflows and recouped only about 0.06% of assets, because the factor was calibrated from rough cost estimates and was small against a multi-day fall ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2112c.htm)). A swing factor is a guess at the cost of a flow. In-kind dealing replaces the guess with the realized cost.

ETFs are the in-kind answer. Authorized participants deliver the basket, bear the trading cost, and the fund never needs a correct price for its own holdings to deal fairly ([Optiver](https://www.optiver.com/insights/explainers/etf-creationredemption-and-authorised-participants)). The stale price does not vanish; it moves into the share's market price, which leads NAV for international ETFs when the underlying market is closed ([WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/us/insights/blog/etfs-people-fear-what-they-dont-understand)) and gapped more than 800 bps from NAV in high-yield bond ETFs in March 2020 ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2112c.htm)). Where assets cannot be priced daily at all, TradFi stops dealing daily: interval funds repurchase 5% to 25% per window on 21 to 42 days' notice ([SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1616037/000161603719000006/ils030519pro497.txt)), and hedge funds use quarterly dealing, 10% to 25% gates and audit holdbacks ([Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/redemption-terms/)).

The on-chain hybrids map onto these tools one for one.

| Hybrid pattern | Live examples | What it fixes | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zap wrapper over in-kind issuance | Index Coop FlashMint, Reserve Zapper | Single-asset UX with slippage on the depositor | Depends on an honest off-chain quote; thin leg caps size; periphery contract risk |
| Async request/claim with forward pricing | Lagoon, Centrifuge, Mellow, Upshift, Makina AsyncRedeemer (ERC-7540) | Removes trading on a known price; Lagoon bars cancellation once the epoch's NAV is proposed ([docs](https://docs.lagoon.finance/developer-hub/integration/async-deposit-flow.md)) | User gets a blind price and a delay; still needs a trustworthy mark at settlement |
| Bounded, delayed accountant rate | Veda (pause on breach, share lock capped at 3 days, refundable deposits) ([Teller source](https://github.com/Se7en-Seas/boring-vault/blob/main/src/base/Roles/TellerWithMultiAssetSupport.sol)) | Turns an accountant error into downtime instead of mispriced flows | Trust in the accountant; staleness between updates |
| Oracle cross-check | Sommelier share-price oracle against TWAP; Mellow deviation classifier; Venus BoundValidator | Single-venue, single-transaction manipulation | Two feeds watching one thin market; agreeing but stale marks |
| Sync while fresh, async when stale | Lagoon `syncDeposit` valid only until `totalAssetsExpiration` ([docs](https://docs.lagoon.finance/developer-hub/integration/synchronous-deposit-flow.md)) | Single-transaction UX during market hours | "There is no slippage parameter"; lifespan is the only protection |
| Swing-style friction | JLP and GLP weight-dependent fees, Veda `sharePremium`, Lagoon haircut, dHEDGE entry and exit fees | Charges the flow an estimate of its cost | Estimate is wrong in stress, as in March 2020 |
| Market-status gate | Chainlink 24/5 streams (`marketStatus` 0 to 5) ([guide](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams/rwa-streams/24-5-us-equities-user-guide.md)) | Deterministic behavior when the reference market is closed | Halts are not reflected; jumps at session boundaries of 1% to 2%, with 10% to 20% tails |

## deSPXA has no honest price for most of the week, so mint by quantity

The recommendation turns on one holding. Chainlink runs push feeds on Base for ETH/USD, BTC/USD and cbBTC/USD with a 20-minute heartbeat and deviation triggers of 0.15%, 0.1% and 0.3%, and all three answered on-chain on 2026-11-11 ([Chainlink directory](https://reference-data-directory.vercel.app/feeds-ethereum-mainnet-base-1.json)). The lending positions and the two crypto assets can therefore be marked. deSPXA cannot. **Chainlink's Base directory of 190 feeds has no S&P 500, SPY, deSPXA or SPXA entry**; its equity feeds cover 13 Coinbase single stocks only, and SPY push feeds exist on Ethereum mainnet, not Base. Chainlink's own equity feeds hold the last close with no heartbeat off-hours ([Chainlink docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/tokenized-equity-feeds/coinbase)), and its weekend-capable products derive a smoothed price from perpetual futures that Chainlink says "can still be manipulated or dislocated during off-hours" ([Chainlink docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams/rwa-streams/24-7-us-equities)). US equities trade 32.5 of 168 hours, leaving about 81% of the week with no primary-session reference price, per a RedStone executive quoted by an aggregator ([OneBullEx](https://www.onebullex.com/news/articles/redstone-coo-warns-tokenized-stocks-lack-live-prices-for-135-5-hours-weekly)). deSPXA itself is a forward-priced, epoch-dealt Centrifuge fund token with daily dealing at NAV for non-US authorized participants ([RWA.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/assets/deSPXA)) and no continuous NAV ([RedStone](https://blog.redstone.finance/2026/04/23/nav-onchain-how-tokenized-fund-pricing-works-and-why-oracle-design-matters/)). Its market cap was about $3.2M in July 2026 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/centrifuge-despxa-morpho-sp500-borrowing/)), and aggregator snapshots of its DEX depth conflict, from one pool of roughly $961K deSPXA and $667K USDC to about $3.8M across pools ([DexPaprika](https://dexpaprika.com/base/token/0x9c5c365e764829876243d0b289733b9d2b729685)).

Test the hardened-NAV proposal against that, component by component. The TWAP would sit on a pool of a few million dollars, the configuration that failed at Inverse and Rari. The independent cross-check has nothing independent to check against on Base, and a second reading of the same thin pool is the Venus failure. The delay converts atomic attacks into multi-block ones but still needs a trustworthy price at settlement, and the only candidate is the issuer's once-daily NAV, whose on-chain contract was not located. The rate limit caps the size of each loss without changing its sign. The result would be a NAV that is predictable from public futures data for most of the week, with holders short a free option sized by the deSPXA weight times the off-hours move. Lenders tolerate a frozen equity mark because a 77% loan-to-value absorbs a 23% gap ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/centrifuge-despxa-morpho-sp500-borrowing/)); a fund has no buffer, and every basis point of mispricing on a flow transfers from holders. The only way to make NAV dealing honest for this portfolio is to adopt the Veda or Lagoon model outright, with an off-chain accountant and queued settlement. That is a defensible product, and it is the steelman for NAV: one-transaction UX, no swap cost on small deposits, bulk deployment by the manager, and the design that holds the TVL. It also puts a trusted pricing role into the flow, leaves new USDC idle until a rebalance, and inherits the two-surface attack profile that produced Makina and Summer.fi.

**Deposits should be quantity-based, and the vault's existing redemption makes that the symmetric choice.** Redemption already pays the realized proceeds of a pro-rata slice, so the redeemer bears exit execution. A deposit that buys a pro-rata slice makes the depositor bear entry execution. With both sides in place, no flow reads a price, holders hold identical quantities per share before and after every flow, and the vault behaves like an ETF whose authorized participant is whoever is transacting. The specific rule proposed, mint by the smallest ratio of quantity received to quantity held, is the Uniswap v2 invariant, and it is safe regardless of how well the purchase went, since a bad swap, a sandwich or a wrong split lowers the depositor's share count and leaves any excess with holders. Three refinements follow from the protocols surveyed. First, measure what arrived as balance deltas and round up on mint and down on redeem, as Uniswap, Set and Folio all do. Second, let the caller supply the per-leg USDC split and a `minSharesOut` floor, with a bound on each leg and not only on the aggregate, so that the on-chain code never needs a price to divide the deposit and the thin leg cannot silently eat the budget as FlashMint's unlimited per-swap input allows. Third, prefer exact-out purchases with a refund of unused USDC, the issuance shape that Set, Folio and RTokens use, so that the minimum rarely binds and composition does not drift through gifted excess. Buying inside the vault also sidesteps the wall that stopped Set and Enzyme: lending positions in Compound and Morpho that are not transferable balances never have to move between accounts, because the vault itself supplies USDC and counts its own position units. Counting those legs in share or scaled units, not rebasing balances, keeps the ratio free of an interest-accrual price; that is this report's inference and needs confirmation in code.

The hybrid elements worth adding are those that never set a share count. An oracle check on the wETH and cbBTC swaps against the Chainlink feeds can revert a deposit whose execution is far from the mark; it protects the depositor and can be switched off without affecting holders. A deposit size cap tied to deSPXA pool depth turns guaranteed reverts into a clear limit. A batched deposit queue, in which requests accumulate and one keeper execution buys the slice for the whole batch when deSPXA liquidity exists, is forward pricing by realized execution: it borrows the ERC-7540 lifecycle and Lagoon's rule against cancelling once settlement starts, without needing anyone to assert a NAV. A secondary DEX pool for the share token gives small users the route Reserve's Zapper already takes when the market is cheaper than minting, and it lets premium or discount, not the vault, carry off-hours price discovery. NAV still has uses outside the flow: display, fee accounting (Folio charges fees as minted shares, which needs no price), and a capped-rate oracle for any lender that lists the share.

The rebalance is where the hardening budget belongs. Quantity-based flows never change weights, so governed rebalancing is the only moment the vault trades at a price someone asserts, and it is the step that cost Indexed Finance $16M. The NAV toolkit applies there in full: minimum-out bounds from Chainlink for wETH and cbBTC, per-rebalance size limits, a delay between proposal and execution, and deSPXA legs executed only in US regular hours or through Centrifuge's primary dealing if the vault or its agent qualifies. Moves between Aave, Compound and Morpho are withdraw-and-supply calls with no market price and suit a restricted rebalancer role better than an auction. Deposits should also read live balances under a reentrancy lock or be blocked while a rebalance is mid-flight; Folio's own source warns that its asset quote "may be unreliable mid-swap" ([Folio.sol](https://github.com/reserve-protocol/reserve-index-dtf/blob/main/contracts/Folio.sol)).

## What remains unverified

Several facts that bear directly on the recommendation were not confirmed. On deSPXA: whether the token carries transfer restrictions that would break in-vault purchase or redemption sales; whether a smart contract can be whitelisted as an authorized participant; the address, update time and signer of Centrifuge's on-chain share price on Base; the dealing cut-off and settlement lag; current supply and pool depth (the latest figure is July 2026); and the oracle used by the Morpho deSPXA market. On oracles: whether Chainlink Data Streams for SPY can be verified on Base, the Pyth contract and feed IDs on Base and whether free access survives Pyth's July 2026 move to paid plans ([Pyth](https://www.pyth.network/blog/extended-hours-us-equity-data-moves-to-pyth-pro)), feed parameters for RedStone, Chronicle and API3, the Base sequencer uptime feed, and whether the three Chainlink proxy addresses read on-chain are the standard feeds or SVR variants. On the design itself: no precedent was found for an in-core single-stablecoin quantity mint, so its rounding, reentrancy and partial-fill behavior have no audited reference; the gas cost of a five-to-eight-leg deposit on Base was not measured; no study quantifies sandwich losses on basket zaps; and the handling of Aave's rebasing aTokens, which can be off by a wei on transfer, was reasoned about and not tested. This report did not inspect the fund's contracts, so the claim that redemption pays realized proceeds of a pro-rata slice rests on the brief.

Parts of the evidence base are also soft. The zero-loss finding for in-kind paths comes from about 35 searches and is affected by low TVL in that class. Class loss totals are the researchers' sums of headline figures. Makina's official post-mortem was not retrieved, and its share-price jump comes from security-firm analyses. The $20,000 to $40,000 rebalance gas figure and the 25 ETH sandwich loss at Index Coop were seen through search summaries. No auction-by-auction results were found for Index Coop or Reserve rebalances. No documented loss from a weekend equity gap was found, which reflects how new tokenized-stock exposure is and not that the risk is absent. No study compares the same portfolio under NAV dealing and in-kind dealing; the comparison here is assembled from different samples and decades. The deSPXA weight in the fund was not given, so the stale-NAV exposure is stated as weight times off-hours move and not as a number.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that "quantity versus NAV" is a question about who holds the option, not about oracles. Under NAV dealing the vault writes an option to every depositor and redeemer on the gap between its mark and the truth, and hardening only shrinks the option. Under quantity dealing the vault writes none, and the transacting party buys execution at whatever the market charges at that moment, including a deSPXA premium on a Sunday. For a portfolio with one asset that nobody prices for most of the week, only the second is honest, and TradFi reached the same answer when it moved hard-to-price exposures into ETFs and interval funds.

Two implications follow. The fund's real pricing risk now sits in the governed rebalance and in the novelty of the in-vault purchase path, so audit effort and the oracle tooling originally imagined for deposits should move there. And the quantity design makes the fund a clean test of a claim the market has not yet settled: index products shrank for want of demand, not because in-kind issuance failed, and a vault that offers one-asset entry with ETF-grade fairness to holders is the version of the experiment that has not been run.
